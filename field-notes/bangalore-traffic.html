Field Note 14 of 12

Bangalore, Karnataka

“Welcome to Bangalore traffic. Expecting a rain.”

Ninety-five minutes. Thirty-five kilometres. An Uber from the airport to the city centre, on a Monday, in a light rain — not a monsoon, not a flood warning, just an ordinary afternoon shower.

The driver was from Bijapur, a few hundred kilometres away. He'd been driving this route long enough that the traffic barely needed comment. Outside the window: two-wheelers weaving around water pooling on roads that had nowhere to send it, a slow crawl in every direction, the pace less like a city and more like a queue.

Overhead, for a long stretch of the ride, an elevated metro line was under construction — the concrete work clean, precise, the kind of quality you notice because you weren't expecting to. Two versions of the same city, visible from the same seat: one being built with real care, a few storeys above the one still waiting its turn.

I asked how bad it usually got. He glanced up at the overcast sky, and answered in Hindi, the words landing somewhere between an apology and a warning.

"Welcome to Bangalore traffic. Expecting a rain."

Not this rain specifically. Rain, as a category. As something the city has simply learned to expect will make things worse, rather than something anyone still expects to be handled.
